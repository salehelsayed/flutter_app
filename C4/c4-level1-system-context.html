1The pieces

PieceWhat it isWhat it does
YouA person Set up an account with a 12-word phrase, show or scan a code to add someone, then chat one to one or in groups.
Your friendA person Uses the same app on their own phone. There is no account for them on any server — they only exist on the phones that added them.
The mknoon appThe thing this drawing is about Runs on iPhone and Android. Holds your keys, your chats and your photos on your own phone, and sends messages straight to your friend's phone, locked so only the two of you can open them.
The helper serverBuilt by the mknoon team Has three small jobs. It introduces two phones so they can find each other. It passes messages along when the two phones cannot reach each other directly. And it holds a message for a while when the other phone is switched off. It cannot open anything it holds.
Google's alert serviceSomeone else's service The way an app is allowed to tap a phone on the shoulder. Works for Android phones and, through Apple, for iPhones too.
Apple's alert serviceSomeone else's service The last step for iPhones. mknoon never talks to it directly — Google's service does.
The phone itselfSomeone else's service Every phone has a built-in safe for secrets. mknoon puts your keys there. The phone also lends the app its camera, its alert banners, and its Wi-Fi.
Health dashboardBuilt by the mknoon team Watches the helper server only, so the team can see it is running. It has no connection to anyone's phone or messages.

3What this drawing tells you

  • There is no company server holding your chats. The helper server only introduces phones and keeps a message for a short while when the other phone is off. It cannot open any of it.
  • Your stuff lives on your phone. Your keys sit in the phone's built-in safe. Your chat history sits in a locked file on the phone.
  • Only two pieces belong to anyone else — the alert services and the phone itself. Both are only used to say "something is waiting". Neither one carries your messages.
  • An alert is a doorbell, not the delivery. The alert only wakes the app. The message itself still comes from your friend's phone, or from the helper server that was holding it.
  • Left out on purpose: what is inside the app, and what is inside the helper server. Those are the next drawings.
