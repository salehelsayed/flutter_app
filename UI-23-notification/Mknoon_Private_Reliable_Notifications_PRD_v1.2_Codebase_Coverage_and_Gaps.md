## 1. Executive finding

Mknoon's notification code contains substantial durability, deduplication, stable-ID, cancellation, native-extension, and recovery machinery. It does **not**, however, implement the central architecture proposed by PRD v1.2.

The current implementation is primarily a **rich encrypted push delivery model**:

- fresh ordinary direct text, fresh ordinary direct media/voice initial chat envelopes, and direct reaction ADD/REMOVE retain sender-owned exact-envelope custody independently of live ACK and use the default-off protected Redis lane's non-destructive capacity and exact ACK-or-expiry proof; Plans 347/348 adopt Plan 346's protected relay-blob primitive for durably prepared ordinary-direct composer/voice, exact reopen-only retry, and marker-free external-OS media -> ordinary-direct sends, preserving byte-identical ciphertext and coupling the v108 envelope lifetime to its strict blobs, but private/disappearing media, edits/deletes, groups/announcements, historical/no-intent rows, device fanout, and production activation still lack the universal PRD contract;
- the relay builds APNs/FCM payloads from message envelopes;
- provider payloads contain routing/event identifiers and encrypted preview material;
- iOS and Android locally decrypt or project that provider-carried material;
- foreground, background, native, and reconciliation paths coordinate through several specialized stores and decision paths.

The PRD requires an **inbox-first opaque-wake model**:

- every notification-worthy event is first committed to the recipient device inbox;
- direct/pubsub delivery is only a fast path for the same authenticated event;
- APNs/FCM receives one fixed generic wake with no sender, conversation, event, or media metadata;
- native processing fetches the inbox and then decrypts, persists, deduplicates, applies policy, and renders locally;
- every entry point uses one fresh lifecycle source, one decision engine, and one atomic notification ledger;
- a transport or persistence acknowledgement cannot cancel the wake without a completed local outcome.

## 8. Migration and compatibility hazards for later plans

### 8.1 Relay/client version skew

Current iOS NSE and Android background processing require rich provider fields. Switching the relay to a generic wake before compatible inbox-fetch clients ship would remove notification resolution for older clients. Plans 342 and 343 introduce distinct current text/edit and reaction-event relay dedupe namespaces. Plan 344 adds separate store/retrieve/ACK actions, exact proof, per-relay negotiation, and a machine-checked S0 old binary → S1 new binary/admission off → S2 admission on → S3 capable client sequence for scoped v108/v109 custody. Plan 345 deliberately reuses v108 and the frozen `direct_text_v108` token for ordinary-media initial chat envelopes, preserving installed Plan 344 relay compatibility. Plan 346 adds optional strict media actions on the existing media protocol, with admission default-off and legacy behavior preserved. Plan 347 makes the first client call those actions only for a complete DB-v111 prepared ordinary-direct composer/voice generation, and its separate client selector also defaults off; pre-v111, partial-legacy, and excluded callers do not promote. These scoped mechanisms do not authorize production admission or solve the later generic-wake migration; future planning must preserve all three rollout controls, authenticated non-push cohort selection, and a pinned rollback artifact.

### 8.2 Current tests lock the old contract

Go and provider-adapter closure tests assert that routing fields survive platform projection and oversized fallback. These are not merely missing target tests; they will actively fail once the PRD privacy contract is implemented. Plans must name which tests are replaced, which remain as compatibility tests, and when legacy fixtures are deleted.

### 8.3 Persisted-state migration

Existing tone claims, recent-remote gates, conversation ID registries, iOS recovery JSON, Android recovery state, and SQLCipher outboxes include Plan 342's DB v108 initial-direct-event custody table and Plan 343's DB v109 reaction custody table. Plan 344 changes only the proof required to retire those rows. Plan 345's DB v110 adds a nullable, local-only manifest intent to `messages` and reuses v108 for complete ordinary-media initial envelopes, with no historical backfill and a deliberate one-way schema floor. Plan 347's DB v111 adds exact-artifact/proof/ACK state and nullable v108 manifest-hash/earliest-expiry binding without promoting historical or no-intent rows; its transfer and cleanup owners preserve identity-scoped artifacts. These states may coexist with the new ledger during rollout. Owner precedence, one-time migration, rollback, account-transfer inventory, and garbage-collection rules need explicit design; future schema plans must preserve pending v108/v109/v111 custody and v110 preparation intent rather than silently recreating, promoting, or omitting them.

### 8.4 Event-identity compatibility

Relay entry IDs, outer routing IDs, decrypted message IDs, reaction transition IDs, and notification event identities are not one uniform authenticated contract today. Plan 342 closes target/edit raw-ID collision for its current text/edit relay paths and authenticates current edit outer/inner identity; direct reactions validate event/action/target parity. Plan 344 additionally binds protected duplicates to recipient, eligible namespaced key, authenticated sender, and byte-identical envelope, but only for the scoped v108/v109 kinds. Those bounded improvements do not establish one universal identity/hash. A ledger migration must not merge distinct events or split one event into multiple alert identities.

### 8.5 Generic fallback ownership

iOS currently sanitizes some unresolved notifications and Android often creates a rich local card from pushed ciphertext. The target requires a generic visible fallback that is retained or updated safely. The transition must prevent both blank notifications and generic-plus-rich double alerts.

### 8.6 Token and environment migration

Moving from peer-keyed provider tokens to opaque handles requires issuance, rotation, revocation, environment binding, multi-device separation, stale-entry cleanup, and compatibility with existing authenticated registration.

### 8.7 Group-mute policy continuity

Rich-payload and future generic-inbox clients must consult the same installation-local group policy during migration. The rollout must not sync mute to sibling devices, widen it to direct/global/timed scope, lose app-icon badge recalculation, resurrect a retired group card, or couple mute to read state. The unused standalone `group_muted:<groupId>` Keychain sentinel needs one explicit disposition: remove/migrate it, or assign and test a single consumer without creating a second mute owner.

### 8.8 PRD rollout gap

PRD v1.2 does not define the full provider-contract rollout. Plan 344 now supplies a narrow relay-custody feature flag, action/proof capability negotiation, old/new sender-receiver-relay behavior, fixed-cardinality metrics, kill-switch drain behavior, and S0-S3 state machine. Canary allocation, production success/error thresholds, actual same-Redis/revision proof, Redis persistence/restart evidence, pinned-artifact rollback, provenance, and the legacy cleanup point remain required before admission or later provider-contract changes.

### 8.9 Protected ACK-or-expiry capacities exist beside legacy eviction contracts

Legacy memory and Redis inbox `store` paths still evict the oldest unacknowledged legacy row at capacity and report `stored`; Plan 344 intentionally freezes that behavior for installed clients. Its Redis-only protected lane instead accepts an exact duplicate before checking capacity and rejects a distinct full-capacity write without deleting protected authority or its legacy shadows. ### 9.1 Adopted test sequencing: Android-first, consolidated iOS closure

Later implementation plans should use this default order:

1. **Implementation phase — GAP-N01 through GAP-N11.** Run focused Go/Dart/SQL/native tests, exact preservation sentinels, affected curated gates, and continuous Android device proof. Use the available physical Android plus emulator for non-iOS two-peer flows. An individual gap does not require an iPhone, iOS UI campaign, or expansion of the physical-iPhone harness as an exit gate.
2. **iOS compile/unit safety during implementation.** Any touched Runner, NSE, App Group, Keychain, or UserNotifications code must compile and pass its focused Swift/native tests before that gap's implementation gate is green. This catches contract and concurrency regressions early without starting the device campaign. It is not a waiver to leave iOS code unbuildable until the end.
3. **Consolidated iOS closure — GAP-N12/WP-07 only.** After GAP-N01 through GAP-N11 are code-complete and their deterministic/native/Android gates are green, resolve the live device matrix and run only the necessary targeted simulator checks plus one bounded physical-iPhone campaign. That campaign owns real APNs-to-NSE behavior, hard Apple lifecycle states, main-app/NSE ledger ownership, communication presentation, device-log privacy, exact delivered-card/badge cleanup, and blank/passive muted-item proof.
4. **Failure loop and final closure.** An iOS failure is a product/test failure, not a reason to broaden the harness by default. Make the smallest production or test correction, rerun the affected focused gates and the bounded iOS scenario, then run final closure. GAP-N12 remains open until this evidence exists or a target is explicitly `N/A (target unavailable by project policy)`.

For this sequencing, “all gaps implemented” means the target production changes for GAP-N01 through GAP-N11 are code-complete and their non-iOS gates are green. GAP-N12 is the proof-closure gap and therefore intentionally remains open while the consolidated iOS phase runs.

This order deliberately defers iOS device integration risk; it does not remove the PRD's iOS obligations. GAP-N01, GAP-N02, GAP-N03, and GAP-N05 normally need no device-specific per-gap gate; GAP-N08 is Android-specific. GAP-N04, GAP-N06, GAP-N07, GAP-N09, GAP-N10, and GAP-N11 may be marked **implementation-complete, iOS evidence-deferred**, but they are not accepted or PRD-compliant until their registered Apple-owned assertions pass in the consolidated GAP-N12 phase.

The existing physical provider adapter should remain a bounded fixture rather than becoming a general framework: it is explicitly a one-payload lifecycle and is coupled to the current rich-payload contract. ### 9.2 Adopted implementation economy and finish-line guardrails

PRD §3.1 is the normative rule. For this roadmap, the objective is to close user-facing privacy and reliability risks, not to maximize plan count, make every path structurally identical or design a universal framework in advance. These are planning rejection rules, not additional release gates.

1. **One coherent vertical slice per plan.** Group tightly coupled outcomes that share an owner and can be proven together. Name the PRD risk or acceptance behavior that changes, the primary production owner and the truthful exclusions. An unused primitive needs a named near-term adopter and does not itself close user-visible acceptance.
2. **Reuse before invention.** Completed authenticated identities, v108/v109 custody, Plan 347's v111 exact-artifact/proof custody, lifecycle retry cadence, notification state, platform adapters and Sims/device fixtures are the default building blocks. A new durable owner must prove a materially different authority, lifetime or atomic transition. A migration, wire change, rollout flag or harness instead needs a concrete compatibility, rollback or otherwise unprovable execution boundary.
3. **No per-modality infrastructure by default.** A different label such as text, media, reaction, edit, delete, private or group is not enough to justify a new owner. Normalize when authority and lifetime match; specialize when evidence shows they do not. Keep downstream work as lightweight dependency/risk outlines until prerequisite interfaces stabilize.
4. **Proportional proof and stop rule.** Use the smallest causal RED set covering every independently changed invariant or failure mode, parameterized edge cases, exact preservation sentinels, the affected curated lane and only justified family gates. No per-plan full `host-all`, duplicate device campaign or routine iOS device leg. Refactoring, fixture consolidation, broad observability, performance tuning and legacy retirement enter the slice only when demonstrated correctness or bounded-performance evidence requires them.
5. **Prefer adoption over score chasing.** Do not accumulate default-off lower-layer primitives within one dependency chain without a production-path adopter. PRD percentage movement is not required per slice; a usable behavior or release-boundary delta is. Finish once the scoped risk and preservation boundary pass, and record unrelated improvements separately.
