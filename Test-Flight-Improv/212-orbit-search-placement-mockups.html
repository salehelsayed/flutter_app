Where should Search live on Orbit?

Finding a friend is one of the two things people do on Orbit — yet its entry is a 44px unlabeled glass circle hiding in the bottom-right corner, the busiest strip of the screen. This exploration relocates and re-weights that affordance: four placements, one recommendation. The expanded search experience (full-width field, live match chips) already works well and is untouched — this is only about the closed, discoverable state: where it sits, how big it is, and what it says.

Today: a key action dressed as furniture

The search entry is 44×44 with a 20px glyph, no label, no border on the rings view — visually the quietest element on a screen full of glowing avatars. And it lives in the one strip that everything else also wants.

  • Corner orphan: on the rings view it floats at right:16, ~104px up — off the natural sightline, unattached to any cluster, easy to read as decoration.
  • Undersized presence: 44px is Apple's bare tap minimum; with dark glass on a dark cosmos and a 20px icon it reads smaller than it hits.
  • Unlabeled: nothing says "find your friends" — icon-only affordances in non-standard positions are the classic discoverability miss.
  • Two homes, one job: the rings view floats it mid-air while the list view tucks a twin beside the nav (~30px up) — same glyph, two different corners.
  • Crowded band: that strip already juggles the nav pill, the edit steppers (198), the chip-strip lift rules (201/205), and the proposed intro dock (207).
  • What already works: the expanded state — full-width field, live avatar chips with ring provenance, matched-node highlighting. The destination is great; the door is hidden.
labeled capsule, dead-center in the thumb zone
Option A design pick
"Find" capsule — bottom-center

The 44px orphan becomes a labeled glass capsule floating centered above the nav band: search glyph + the word "Find", 48px tall. Tap → it stretches into the existing full-width search bar, in place. Same widget, new closed state.

Why it works
  • Thumb-zone bullseye: bottom-center is the easiest one-handed reach on any hand, any size phone — and RTL-neutral for free.
  • A label is the affordance: "Find" answers what does this do before the tap; discoverability jumps without any onboarding.
  • Morph, don't teleport: the capsule expands into the full-width bar it already becomes today — one continuous element, no new mental model.
  • One home on both surfaces: the identical capsule sits in the identical spot on the rings and the list view, replacing both 44px twins.
Watch out
  • Shares the under-rings band with 207's proposed intro dock — if that ships, the dock rides above the capsule (stacked), or the two fuse into one bar.
  • Hides during sculpt-edit like all orbit chrome; keeps today's lift rules when armed.
  • Keep it a capsule, not a full-width bar — the rings must stay the hero.

Option B runner-up
Fused into the nav pill

The glass nav pill grows a third slot: Feed · Orbit · | · search, separated by a hairline divider. Search becomes a first-class citizen of the one piece of chrome users already touch constantly.

Why it works
  • Zero orphans: the whole bottom strip collapses into a single glass cluster — calmer screen, no floating mystery circles.
  • Learned once, found forever: the nav pill is the most-touched, most-looked-at element in the app; anything inside it is discovered by osmosis.
  • Familiar pattern: search-in-the-tab-bar is a pattern users bring from Instagram / App Store — no teaching needed.
  • Stable across surfaces: the pill (and its search slot) is identical on rings and list views by construction.
Watch out
  • Search is an action, not a destination — the divider + circular slot (no label, no active state) must keep it reading as a button, never a third tab.
  • Pill widens ~55px; verify the Orbit badge and RTL spacing still breathe.
  • On the rings view the tap opens the find bar; on the list view, the search dock — one slot, two docks to wire.

Option C most conventional
Top chrome — beside create

Search moves to the top row where users expect it: a 40px glass circle next to the + FAB, matching the view toggle's vocabulary. The bottom strip empties completely.

Why it works
  • Convention: two decades of apps put search top-right; users scan there first when they want to find something.
  • Frees the bottom band entirely for the nav, the edit steppers, and a future intro dock — the collision choreography (lift rules) mostly disappears.
  • Symmetry: toggle left · search + create right — a balanced, legible chrome row in the existing 40px vocabulary.
Watch out
  • Reach: top-right is the worst one-handed zone on 6.1–6.7" phones — the most-used verb ends up in the hardest spot. The expanded field would also anchor top while the keyboard is bottom.
  • Still icon-only, still 40px — solves position, not presence.
  • Three glass circles up top begins to crowd; the + FAB's glow must stay the loudest (creation > search in that row).

Option D max discoverability
Persistent search bar — top

The messaging-app classic: a slim, always-visible "Find friends…" field pinned under the top chrome. Nothing to discover — the search UI simply is there, like WhatsApp or Telegram.

Why it works
  • Zero discovery cost: the placeholder text is permanent instruction; even first-run users can't miss it.
  • No morph choreography: tap, keyboard up, type — the closed and open states are the same element.
  • Doubles as a header, giving the rings view the top anchor it currently lacks.
Watch out
  • Permanent tax for an occasional verb: ~56px of vertical is spent forever on something used a few times a day; the rings shift down and the cosmos loses its emptiness — the screen's whole identity.
  • Fights the minimal, chrome-less aesthetic the Orbit surface is built on; heaviest visual change of the four.
  • Top field + bottom keyboard = maximum finger travel during use.

recommendation

A — a labeled capsule in the thumb zone; B if the bottom band gets crowded

Ship Option A. It fixes all three complaints at once — position (corner → bottom-center thumb zone), size (44px circle → 48px labeled capsule with real presence), and discoverability (icon-only → the word "Find") — while reusing the morph the pill already performs: the capsule is just a better closed state for the exact widget that exists today, on both surfaces. B is the strong runner-up and the right fallback if the under-rings band gets claimed by the 207 intro dock: fusing search into the nav pill also kills the orphan and is trivially consistent across surfaces, at the cost of a subtler action-vs-tab distinction. C is the safe conventional answer but moves the most-used verb to the hardest-to-reach zone and keeps it icon-only — it solves where, not what. D buys discoverability the expensive way, permanently spending the emptiness that makes the Orbit screen feel like space. Whichever ships, both of today's 44px twins retire together — one search affordance, one home, both surfaces.

One affordance, two docks

The rings view opens the in-place find bar (chips + node highlighting, inner_circle_interactive_surface.dart:928); the list view opens the search dock (orbit_search_dock.dart). The new entry replaces both triggers but keeps each surface's expanded behavior.

Edit & band etiquette

Sculpt-edit hides all orbit chrome — the new entry hides too. Armed-edit lift rules (TC-198F-17/18) and the 201 bottomClearance floor carry over unchanged for A/B; C/D exit the band entirely.

RTL & a11y

Top/bottom-center placements are RTL-neutral; C follows the physical-corner rule (orbit_view_toggle_button.dart:9-12). All options keep ≥44pt targets and the existing orbit_find_pill_semantics label; A adds a visible one.

Standalone mode

When Orbit runs without the persistent nav (X-to-close mode), A's capsule keeps bottom-center above the close button; B falls back to A's capsule since there is no nav pill to fuse into.
