question 1 · decided trigger, open geometry

Tap the +N badge — how do the arches open?

The badge becomes the toggle: it gets a real seat (ring 2 re-lays as 9 slots when overflow exists, so nothing crowds), a 44pt hit area, and flips to a chevron while open. What differs between the options is the shape the overflow takes above the circle.

try: tap the +N badge · then the chevron
Option A safest ship
Stacked Domes

Orbit3's arch rows, production-hardened. Overflow chunks into gentle domes of 7 that stack upward, newest nearest the circle. Same math the lab already proved — plus the fixes it never got.

Why it works
  • Densest option — 7 per row; 50 friends = 6 rows, all visible on one screen at default scale.
  • Row pitch raised 50→56px so every avatar honors the 44pt tap floor the inner circle already enforces.
  • No row is ever culled: the circle glides down to make room and the whole field scrolls — arches and circle both stay reachable (the lab marooned the circle below the fold with no way back).
  • 194 unread satellites ride along onto arch members — the lab dropped them.
Watch out
  • Reads as rows of people, not orbits — the dome dip (11.5px) is subtle; the ring metaphor weakens up top.
  • Name labels stay off by default; double-tap toggles them, staggered two-row under alternate seats — the lab's 9.5px labels collided with the next row.

Option B design pick
Concentric Arcs

The orbit simply keeps going. Overflow rides true arcs — r154, r200, r246, the same +46px ring gap — sharing the circle's center, drawn in the same dashed teal/purple vocabulary. The +N badge doesn't open a panel; it reveals the rest of the solar system.

Why it works
  • One geometry, one metaphor — recency still reads as distance from you, continuously, instead of switching to rows at seat 14.
  • The circle takes its expanded seat instantly — no glide — and the outer arcs bloom around it with staggered entrances; the motion belongs to the arrivals, not the circle.
  • Arc capacity self-limits (~9/8/8 within screen width), which keeps every tap target ≥44pt for free — and the per-arc knob can cap a ring lower (fewer seats, spread wider), pushing the rest outward.
  • The curve knob becomes “wrap”: from a shallow crown (72° per side) all the way to arcs that reach 166° per side — a 332° shell. Past 1.6× the width pinch releases and the arcs bend on around the circle; at full wrap the overflow is a true spiral shell around your inner circle.
Watch out
  • Lower density: ~8–9 seats per arc, so 50+ friends stack five-plus arcs — the circle slides down to fit them and the canvas scrolls to travel the stack.
  • Edge avatars sit close to the bezel — and past 1.6× wrap they deliberately clip at it (the pinch sin φ ≤ 170/r holds only up to 1.6×). Fine as a sculpted look; the default must stay fully on-screen on narrow devices.

question 2 · long-press replaces always-on chrome

Hold the orbit to tune it

Orbit3 pins its four knobs on screen permanently. Here the canvas stays clean: a long-press (500ms) anywhere on the orbit enters edit mode — haptic, rings brighten — and one of these three control surfaces appears. Same four dimensions, same ranges, persisted like the lab (SecureKeyStore). Done or tap-outside exits.

Option A design pick
Tune Dock

A glass dock slides over the nav with four labeled sliders and live values. The orbit redraws as you drag — the whole canvas stays visible above it.

Why it works
  • Sliders fix the lab's coarse steppers — continuous scrub, real labels, live value readout.

  • One save on Done (the lab wrote to Keychain on every tap).
Watch out
  • Covers the nav pill while open (it's modal by intent).

Option B
Orbit Handles

No panel at all. Edit mode puts grab handles on the geometry itself: pull the outer ring to space the rings and the arcs, the seat handle to size avatars, the arc tip to wrap the overflow around the circle — all the way to the 332° shell — and the tip's twin on the far end to cap how many seats each arc holds. (On the domes variant the pair becomes dome-bend + per-row count.)

Why it works
  • Zero chrome — you sculpt the thing you're looking at; the mapping teaches itself.
  • Entering edit dims the crowd to 22% so the four handles pop; the dim lifts while you drag, so the orbit answers your hand at full brightness, then settles back.
  • Tap a handle to arm it: its glow flips green → teal, its live value floats in a bubble right above it, and −/+ buttons land on the nav-bar line (− left, + right) — both thumbs, no reaching, nothing covering the orbit. Drag stays available for coarse moves.
  • Feels native to the orbital metaphor; the jiggle + handles read instantly as "edit mode".
Watch out
  • Discoverability + a11y: four drag targets need alternate switch-access paths; VoiceOver story is hard.
  • Wrap/max-per-arc handles only exist when the arches are open — two invisible controls otherwise.

Option A design pick
Come To Me

Don't hunt — summon. The best match glides out of the overflow to a spotlit pedestal beneath the circle: full-size, named, one tap from chat. Everyone else dims. Clear the query and they fly home.

Why it works

Option C
Result Chips

As you type, every on-canvas match lights in place while the crowd fades to 28%, and the matching faces appear as a chip strip above the pill — avatar, name, and where they live ("ring 1", "arc 2"). Tap a chip to focus one match or open the chat. The orbit itself never moves.

recommendation

B-arcs for the product · A-dock to tune it · A-summon to find them

Arches: Option B (Concentric Arcs) is the design pick — it makes overflow feel like more orbit instead of a different widget, and its capacity limit buys correct tap targets for free. Option A (Stacked Domes) is the proven, denser fallback if 50+ friend density on one screen outranks the metaphor. Edit: Option A (Tune Dock) — sliders + labels + live preview fix every documented stepper complaint; C is the budget path (gate the existing lab widget behind long-press); B (Handles) is a delight worth keeping on the shelf, but its a11y story shouldn't block this feature. Find: Option A (Come To Me) — summoning beats hunting at every population size, and it composes: B's glow-in-context can fire when the arches happen to be open, and C's chip strip is the natural upgrade for multi-match queries. Documented as considered-and-declined: a pull-up drawer (list, not orbit — rejected with InnerSky) and fisheye tier-climbing (novel gesture load for a find task).

Chrome untouched

193 toggle (top-left), 196 QR pair (top-center), FAB (top-right), nav pill — all keep their seats in every option. The arches live strictly between the chrome band and the nav; the expanded canvas clips beneath both.

Badge fix rides along

Whatever wins, the +N badge gets a real 9th slot on ring 2 (8 members re-spread across 9 seats on overflow), a 44pt hit target, chevron-when-open, and Semantics "N more people — tap to open".

Unread + groups everywhere

Arch members are the same node species as ring members: 194 unread satellites, 197 group avatars (teal border), and tap-to-open all carry up into the arches. The lab rendered arch members bare.

Persistence & RTL

Four knobs persist via the SecureKeyStore key + use-case pattern (background_preference template) and must register in the migration registry or they won't survive account Move. Arch fill order mirrors under RTL — the lab laid out LTR-only.
