Unread, on the orbit

user-b has sent messages user-a hasn't read. Four ways to say so on the inner-circle map, each running live below on the real Orbit geometry — first as a close-up on user-b alone, then at scale with 13 friends, four of them with unread messages. Tap any avatar to read and watch the signal clear — exactly what happens when the chat is opened from the Orbit, a notification, or the Feed.

Close-up · the signal itself

user-b alone on the inner ring, as in the screenshot. Judge the shape and the read-decay here.

A

Count badge

The app's existing green unread pill, moved onto the orbital node. Zero motion, carries the exact count, instantly familiar from the friend list. The quietest honest option.

B

Messenger orbit

The contact-profile orbit you love, miniaturized: same 1.1px ring, same halo'd satellites and phases, sped from 28s to 9s so it reads as activity. One satellite per unread message (caps at 3). No numerals — a message is literally circling user-b, waiting.

tap user-b to read

C

Orbit + badge

Motion for the glance, badge for the count: a single satellite carries the ambient signal while the pill answers "how many". The loudest option — earns its keep if unread counts matter on this surface.

tap user-b to read

D

Breathing beacon

No new geometry at all: a thin accent ring hugs the avatar and the glow breathes on a slow 2.4s cycle. Ambient and calm, but carries no count and is the easiest to miss at a glance.

tap user-b to read

At scale · a full inner circle

13 friends placed with the app's real formula (5 × 38px on the inner ring, 8 × 30px staggered +15° on the outer). Four have unread messages — user-b at the top with 2, plus friends with 1, 3 and 1. This is where the alternatives really separate: watch how much motion the sky can take, whether badges collide, and whether you can still find "who wants me" in half a second. Tap any lit avatar to read that friend (clears in all four cards); "a message arrives" lights up a random friend.

A

Count badge

Badges survive density best and stay countable — but four sets of numerals makes the sky read like a dashboard.

B

Messenger orbit

The judgement call: every lit friend gets motion. Four orbits at once is lively — decide here if it's alive or noisy. (All satellites share one angular velocity, so the motion stays coherent.)

C

Orbit + badge

Loudest at scale — motion and numerals on four nodes. If this feels like too much, a hybrid rule helps: satellite always, badge only when count > 1.

D

Breathing beacon

Calmest crowd by far — but with four beacons breathing on the same cycle it reads as ambience, not as "these four specifically want you".

Recommendation · C on this screen, B if counts stay in the list

C (orbit + badge) gives both answers a glance needs — who (the motion pulls the eye) and how much (the pill) — and both halves are already app vocabulary: the satellite ring is the contact-profile orbit, the pill is the friend-row unread badge. If the at-scale section feels loud, B is the elegant fallback: message-as-satellite is the most "orbit-native" metaphor here.

Accent: green is the app's existing unread semantic (friend rows); red currently means tab-level attention. Recommend green for consistency — flip the swatches above to compare.

Implementation note: this is presentation-only. OrbitFriend.unreadCount already exists and is populated via getUnreadCountForContact; reading from chat, notification, or Feed already zeroes it, so the clear-on-read behavior shown here comes for free on the next projection update.
